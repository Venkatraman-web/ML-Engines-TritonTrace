================================================================================
PART 1. THE PROBLEM & AVOIDED PITFALLS
================================================================================

We have a single Sentinel-1 satellite observation of an oil slick at t0, and
hourly Copernicus MEDSEA ocean-current data and ERA5 wind data going back
168 hours.

The objective is to estimate a spatio-temporal posterior density:

    p(x_s, y_s, t_s, V | y)

where:

    x_s, y_s = source location
    t_s      = source time
    V        = source magnitude/volume parameter
    y        = observed satellite pixel data

The system should ultimately produce a time-indexed set of posterior modes
and uncertainty regions rather than forcing the inference into a single
deterministic source point.

WHY SIMPLE REVERSE TRAJECTORIES ARE INSUFFICIENT
------------------------------------------------

Do NOT implement naive deterministic backtracking as the inference engine.

Important issues include:

1. BACKWARD DIFFUSION

The backward heat equation is mathematically ill-posed. Therefore, simply
reversing a forward diffusion model does not provide a physically valid way
to reconstruct the source probability distribution.

2. FLOW DIVERGENCE / COMPRESSION

The ocean surface velocity field can be strongly divergent or compressible
because of currents, windage, and Ekman-related processes.

A naive reverse trajectory ensemble can therefore accumulate in regions of
strong flow convergence. Such accumulation can produce artificial density
peaks that do not necessarily correspond to a physical spill source.

3. CLEAN-WATER CONSTRAINTS

A satellite image contains both oil-positive and oil-negative pixels.

A source hypothesis should therefore be evaluated not only by whether it can
produce oil in observed oil pixels, but also by whether it is consistent with
observed clean-water pixels.

4. STRUCTURAL FORCING UNCERTAINTY

Errors that coherently translate or deform the simulated slick cannot be
represented adequately by simply increasing particle-level random diffusion.

Wind/current forcing uncertainty must therefore be represented explicitly
through an ensemble of perturbed forcing fields.

================================================================================
PART 2. CORE ENGINE: WEIGHTED BACKWARD ADJOINT
================================================================================

The transport problem is treated using Source-Receptor Duality.

For each observation pixel, including both oil-positive pixels and an
appropriate buffer of clean pixels, particles are released and propagated
backward from the observation time t0.

Define backward time:

    theta = t0 - t

The proposed backward stochastic differential equation for the Stage III
implementation is:

    dX* = (-U + grad(K)) dtheta + sqrt(2K) dB

STAGE III V1 ASSUMPTIONS
------------------------

For the first implementation:

1. U(x,t) is the horizontal ocean-surface velocity field after all selected
   deterministic transport components (including any explicitly modeled
   windage contribution) have been combined.

2. K(x,t) is a scalar, horizontally isotropic diffusivity.

3. grad(K) is therefore the spatial gradient of the scalar diffusivity.

4. B is a 2-dimensional Brownian motion.

5. Tensor/anistropic diffusivity may be considered in a later version but
   should NOT be implemented in Stage III v1 unless explicitly requested.

6. The model operates on the horizontal geographic domain represented by
   the supplied current/wind data.

7. Land/coast boundaries must be handled explicitly. Particles must not
   silently propagate through land. The implementation should use a clearly
   documented boundary policy (e.g. rejection/reflection or coastline
   clipping) rather than silently dropping particles.

8. Particles leaving the valid ocean/data domain must be marked invalid and
   excluded according to the documented validity policy.

WEIGHT CORRECTION
-----------------

Because the velocity field may locally compress or expand particle volume,
each particle carries a dynamic weight w.

The proposed backward weight evolution is:

    d(ln w) / dtheta = - div(U)

This correction is intended to account for the compression/expansion of the
probability-density measure under the chosen forward/backward transport
operator.

IMPORTANT:

Do NOT describe this equation as an unconditional guarantee of probability
mass conservation.

Its validity depends on the precise transport operator, stochastic
convention, diffusivity definition, boundary conditions, and numerical
discretization.

The implementation must therefore:

- calculate div(U) consistently with the same velocity field U used by the
  transport model;
- update particle weights consistently with the chosen time-stepping scheme;
- preserve/document the sign convention for backward time;
- provide diagnostics for total weighted mass;
- allow mass-conservation behavior to be tested numerically on controlled
  velocity fields.

A key purpose of this correction is to prevent raw particle accumulation
caused by flow compression from being interpreted automatically as increased
source probability.

IMPORTANT DISTINCTION
---------------------

A high particle/KDE concentration is NOT by itself proof of a physical source.

Concentration may arise from:

- an actual source,
- deterministic flow convergence,
- diffusivity gradients,
- structural forcing errors,
- boundary effects,
- or Monte Carlo sampling noise.

Therefore, the Stage III particle density must be treated as a transport
probability estimate, not as an automatic source detector.

MONTE CARLO INTERPRETATION
--------------------------

The adjoint particle system does NOT produce an exact probability.

It produces a Monte Carlo estimate of the transport probability.

Use notation such as:

    c_hat*_{j,e}(x,t)

rather than describing the lookup table as an exact probability.

With finite particle number, the estimate contains sampling error. Particle
count, effective sample size, and invalid-particle fraction should therefore
be tracked as diagnostics.

NUMERICAL INTEGRATION
---------------------

Stage III v1 should use an explicitly documented Euler-Maruyama-style
discretization of the specified SDE unless a more appropriate scheme is
demonstrated and explicitly approved.

The implementation must clearly document:

- time-step size;
- spatial interpolation method for U and K;
- treatment of missing data;
- boundary handling;
- random-number generation;
- particle-weight update;
- invalid-particle handling.

PART 3. FULL INFERENCE PIPELINE (STAGES I - VII)
================================================================================

STAGE I. OBSERVATION OPERATOR
-----------------------------

Rasterize the satellite observation into pixels A_j.

Let:

    y_j = 1  if oil is detected
    y_j = 0  if the pixel is classified as clean

Define a wind-dependent detection probability:

    pi_j = P(y_j = 1 | h_j)

where h_j is the predicted oil thickness or corresponding observable
quantity.

The observation model should account for the fact that:

- high winds can mix oil below the surface and reduce radar contrast;
- sufficiently calm conditions can also reduce detectability;
- classification is uncertain.

The exact functional form of pi_j must be explicitly specified in the
implementation rather than invented silently.

STAGE II. FORCING ENSEMBLE (STRUCTURAL UNCERTAINTY)
----------------------------------------------------

Create E perturbed wind/current forcing fields, with E approximately 50 for
the initial implementation.

These perturbations represent coherent spatial/temporal forcing errors.

They should NOT simply be replaced by additional independent particle
diffusion.

The purpose is to represent uncertainty such as:

- coherent current displacement;
- coherent wind bias;
- spatially correlated forcing errors;
- temporally correlated forcing errors.

Each forcing ensemble member e must remain internally coherent.

The exact perturbation model should be documented, including its covariance
or perturbation mechanism.

STAGE III. WEIGHTED BACKWARD ADJOINT LOOKUP TABLE
--------------------------------------------------

Run the weighted backward adjoint for all E forcing ensemble members.

For each observation pixel j and forcing member e, store the Monte Carlo
transport estimate over source location and time.

Conceptually:

    c_hat*_{j,e}(x,t)

represents the estimated probability contribution that oil originating at
candidate source state (x,t), under forcing scenario e, could contribute to
observation pixel j.

This is a Monte Carlo approximation, NOT an exact probability.

Particle states should be saved at approximately 15-30 minute intervals,
using spatial hashing or another memory-efficient indexing mechanism.

Each stored particle record should retain at minimum:

- source/backward time;
- location;
- particle weight;
- observation-pixel label;
- forcing ensemble member;
- validity state.

Diagnostics should include:

- total particles;
- valid particles;
- invalid particles;
- total particle weight;
- effective sample size where applicable.

STAGE IV. SURROGATE LIKELIHOOD
------------------------------

Millions of source hypotheses will be evaluated using the Stage III lookup
table.

CRITICAL MATHEMATICAL REQUIREMENT:

The spatial correlation tempering exponent beta MUST be applied to the
MARGINALIZED probability mixture, NOT independently inside the forcing
ensemble product.

The required formulation is:

    p_tilde(y | Theta, V)
        =
    [ (1/E) sum_{e=1}^E
        product_{j=1}^J P(y_j | h_{j,e})
    ]^beta

where:

    P(y_j | h_{j,e})
      =
    (1-epsilon) * pi_{j,e}^{y_j}
        * (1-pi_{j,e})^(1-y_j)
      + epsilon/2

The exponent beta is applied AFTER the ensemble likelihoods have been
combined into the marginalized mixture.

Do NOT move beta inside the ensemble sum/product.

Do NOT replace this mixture likelihood with an average of independently
tempered ensemble likelihoods.

The purpose of the formulation is to preserve the intended forcing-mixture
interpretation while tempering overconfidence caused by spatial correlation
between pixels.

STAGE V. GLOBAL SAMPLING
------------------------

1. GRID SCREEN

Evaluate the surrogate likelihood on a coarse space-time grid.

The purpose is to identify all regions with non-negligible posterior support
rather than only searching near a single maximum.

2. TEMPERED SEQUENTIAL MONTE CARLO (SMC)

Initialize SMC particles from the screened source space.

Move them through a sequence of tempered target distributions so that
multiple posterior modes can be explored.

The system must be able to preserve distinct modes caused by phenomena such
as tidal aliases or forcing ambiguity.

Do not collapse all posterior modes into one "best point" during this stage.

STAGE VI. FULL-PHYSICS CORRECTION
---------------------------------

The Stage III adjoint is linear and computationally efficient.

The physical forward model may be nonlinear because of effects such as:

- thickness-dependent evaporation;
- Fay-type spreading;
- nonlinear drift;
- other selected oil-weathering processes.

The SMC posterior therefore requires correction using the full nonlinear
forward model.

CRITICAL ARCHITECTURE REQUIREMENT:

Do NOT replace this stage with ordinary Importance Sampling/Reweighting.

Binary spatial observations can produce catastrophic importance-weight
variance and an effective sample size approaching 1.

Use Delayed-Acceptance MCMC (DA-MCMC).

DA-MCMC procedure:

1. Propose a source candidate using the proposal mechanism associated with
   the current surrogate posterior state.

2. Evaluate the fast surrogate posterior.

3. Apply the first acceptance/rejection stage using the surrogate model.

4. ONLY if the proposal passes the surrogate screening stage, execute the
   computationally expensive full nonlinear forward simulation.

5. Evaluate the full-physics likelihood.

6. Apply the final acceptance/rejection correction so that the resulting
   chain targets the intended full-physics posterior.

The implementation must preserve the distinction between:

- surrogate target;
- full-physics target;
- first-stage acceptance;
- second-stage acceptance.

Do not silently turn DA-MCMC into simple surrogate reweighting.

STAGE VII. AIS-READY POSTERIOR OUTPUT
-------------------------------------

The system must NOT output only a single "best point".

The posterior should be formatted into a time-indexed source corridor suitable
for downstream AIS matching.

For EVERY historical checkpoint (for example, every hour), extract the
highest-posterior-density (HPD) modes.

If multiple distinct posterior modes remain because of tidal aliases,
forcing ambiguity, or other identifiable causes, output multiple modes for
that timestamp.

The target JSON structure is:

{
  "timestamp": "YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SSZ",
  "spill_age_hours": 24.0,
  "status": "resolved",
  "candidate_lat": 32.41,
  "candidate_lon": 31.72,
  "peak_density": 0.84,
  "hpd50_area_km2": 63.2,
  "hpd90_area_km2": 210.4,
  "convergence_index": 3.4,
  "valid_particle_fraction": 0.96,
  "mode_id": 1
}

IMPORTANT: CONVERGENCE INDEX
----------------------------

The convergence_index field MUST NOT be treated as a source-probability
guarantee.

A precise mathematical definition must be established before using this field
for classification.

The initial implementation should treat convergence_index as a diagnostic
quantity describing posterior/ensemble concentration, not as independent
proof of source validity.

The metric must distinguish, where possible, between:

- concentration caused by source-consistent transport;
- concentration caused by flow compression;
- concentration caused by forcing structure;
- concentration caused by insufficient particle sampling.

The system must NOT define:

    high convergence_index => resolved source

unless that relationship has been explicitly validated.

The "resolved" status should depend on the posterior structure and validation
criteria, not on KDE peak height alone.

VALID PARTICLE FRACTION
-----------------------

valid_particle_fraction must represent the fraction of particles remaining
valid under the documented domain/boundary/data-validity rules.

A low valid-particle fraction should be treated as a diagnostic warning and
should prevent overconfident interpretation of the corresponding posterior
mode.
